**What changed.** A dead lane used to wait for Jo's word. Now, on an autonomous workload, a lane that died (an unintended exit, a signal stop such as an as2 restart, a lapsed lease) while the workload has an open unpaused slice and no open question is relaunched by the drain or tick, within the lane cap and in rank order. The launch cause is `relaunch <dead lane> <n>`. Relaunch 1 is immediate; relaunches 2 and 3 wait 10 minutes (`RELAUNCH_BACKOFF_MS`) after the replaced death's `lane-died:` message (refusal `relaunch-backoff`). After `RETRY_LIMIT` = 3 relaunches without progress (a new slice, a slice done, a new question, a `done-because:`, a new artifact tree; the test runner's full-suite slice is not progress) the surface writes exactly one `incident: relaunch <wl> key <lane> after 3/3 retries without progress: …` message, shown as one unnumbered needs-you line naming Jo's choices: relaunch with `orchestrator_launch_lane`, close, or instruct. The workload then waits for Jo (refusal `relaunch-exhausted`). The count resets on progress and on Jo's word. Parked (a paused slice or an open question), done (`done-because:`), Jo-stopped (`lane-stop … by jo` or the runner's `accepted ceiling`; refusal `stopped`) and user-driven workloads are never relaunched, and the merge-train workload is excluded. A machinery request (a train task) for a dead lane the relaunch does not take on launches as a `request`, as before. A lapsed lease whose recorded runner pid is still alive is not a death (`laneEnd` reads it `live`; refusal `held`). A `launch-requested: by jo` older than a stop by Jo or the ceiling never launches. While a relaunch takes a death on, its lane-died and idle lines are hidden. A restart's signal stop now writes its `lane-died:` with the acceptance, and a lapsed lease gives one death per lane. On a train-owned workload an owner death the relaunch takes on is not a train attempt, and while the incident stands the train holds the owner's PRs, tasks given after the incident included (`train #<n> held` leads the incident's detail).
